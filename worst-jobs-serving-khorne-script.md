# The Worst Jobs Serving Khorne (Ranked by How Little Mercy the Blood God Actually Shows His Own)

Every other Chaos God offers its followers something resembling a genuine bargain, knowledge in exchange for devotion to Tzeentch, pleasure without limit to Slaanesh, patient, eventual survival to Nurgle. Khorne offers exactly one single thing in return for a mortal's entire loyalty, and unlike every other option currently available anywhere across the Warp's own crowded marketplace of competing patrons, this particular offer isn't negotiable in any direction whatsoever, take it exactly as given or don't bother approaching the Blood God's throne at all. Blood, delivered honestly, in open combat, without a single trace of the sorcery or cowardice the Blood God despises above every other failing a mortal servant could possibly commit. Khorne famously cares not from whence the blood flows, a philosophy his own followers repeat to each other like scripture, but he cares enormously, obsessively about exactly how that blood actually gets spilled, and that single, brutal distinction between the two is the entire reason serving him tends to be considerably less forgiving than serving any other power currently operating in the Warp. Unlike his three rival Chaos Gods, Khorne has never once bothered establishing anything resembling a formal priesthood, a settled hierarchy, or even a consistent theology beyond the handful of rules every follower learns almost immediately, fight honestly, never retreat, and never once resort to sorcery no matter how badly the alternative might tempt you in a losing battle. There are no temples built in his honor anywhere in realspace or the Warp, no priesthood managing his affairs from anywhere resembling comfort or safety. Every battlefield anywhere in the galaxy already counts as sacred ground the instant fighting actually begins there, every massacre already counts as genuine worship regardless of who ordered it or why, and the only prayer most of his followers ever bother offering across an entire lifetime of service is a single word bellowed at full volume mid-charge, no incense, no ritual, no formal liturgy required or even particularly welcomed. Here are seven ways to serve the Blood God without ever earning a place among his favored champions, ranked from "brutal but survivable" to "there was never any version of this that ended well," worst saved for last.

**Number 1: Cell Cultist**

Khorne's mortal worship on Imperial worlds operates through deliberately cellular structures specifically designed to survive the constant, grinding pressure of Imperial persecution, small, isolated cells ranging anywhere from a handful of members to a few hundred, most of whom will never once, across an entire lifetime of service, meet anyone from the wider cult beyond their own immediate, tightly controlled circle. A cell cultist's actual daily existence involves considerably more careful waiting and quiet secrecy than open, satisfying bloodshed, patiently recruiting the desperate and the traumatized, men and women who lost a close friend to a bar fight or an entire family to an Arbites riot suppression action and simply never found a better, more socially acceptable outlet for the rage that inevitably followed. Only after months, sometimes years of this quiet, patient groundwork does the cell finally, slowly begin building toward whatever violent offering its own leadership has actually planned, a slow-burning fuse most members never see the far end of before the operation, one way or another, finally concludes. The genuine, day-to-day danger here isn't Khorne's own personal displeasure, since a low-level cultist deep inside a single cell rarely gets close enough to the Blood God's actual, direct attention to ever meaningfully earn it either way. The real threat is simple discovery, since an Imperium that treats suspected heresy as grounds for immediate, entirely unquestioned execution has never once, in ten thousand years of zealous enforcement, shown the slightest genuine interest in distinguishing an enthusiastic new recruit barely a week into their service from a decades-long, deeply committed true believer standing right beside them in the same cramped cellar.

**Number 2: Master of Executions**

Somebody within every serious Khorne warband has to actually judge, trophy by bloody trophy, whether a given kill is genuinely worthy enough to count toward the wider tally headed eventually for the Skull Throne itself, and that specific, unenviable job falls to whoever has earned enough hard-won trust among the warband's own veteran killers to handle the counting without getting personally, permanently killed over an unpopular disagreement about the final results. A Master of Executions who serves long enough learns to read the particular gleam in a berserker's eye that means the disagreement is about to stop being verbal. Khorne's own stated preference, according to the handful of surviving cult texts anyone has ever bothered writing down, favors the skulls of mighty champions, dangerous monsters, and genuine enemy leaders considerably over the indiscriminate, unremarkable slaughter of the defenseless, at least in careful theory. A Master of Executions' actual, practical function is separating the offerings genuinely worth formally presenting from the ones a berserker simply grabbed off whatever unlucky corpse happened to be closest during the heat of the frenzy itself. In practice, this careful distinction collapses constantly and predictably, since berserkers mid-rampage rarely pause mid-swing to consult anyone about whose head they happen to be currently removing from its shoulders. This leaves the Master of Executions in the genuinely unenviable position of either accepting an unworthy offering and quietly risking the entire warband's standing with the Blood God, or rejecting it outright and personally, directly explaining that unwelcome decision to the exact kind of warrior who solved his last serious disagreement with an axe rather than a conversation.

**Number 3: Berzerker-Surgeon**

The Apothecaries of the World Eaters Traitor Legion, known across the wider Chaos underworld as Berzerker-Surgeons or Butcher-Surgeons, inherited a genuinely gruesome specialty from the Legion's own founding trauma, the surgical implantation of the Butcher's Nails, cortical devices that heighten aggression and pain tolerance at the direct cost of every other emotion a warrior might otherwise feel outside of combat. With the Legion's own gene-seed stocks badly depleted after ten thousand years of continuous, grinding warfare, modern Berzerker-Surgeons have adapted by harvesting genetic material from other Space Marines entirely, forcibly implanting the resulting organs into captured Initiates who never once volunteered for any part of the process, before finally, if the candidate somehow survives that first brutal ordeal, moving on to the Nails themselves. The original implantation technique under Apothecary Gahlan Surlak carried a documented one hundred percent fatality rate before it was ever properly refined, and even the considerably more reliable modern version remains a brutal, hands-on surgical ordeal performed by a specialist who has to somehow keep a half-finished, screaming patient stable and alive while surrounded by exactly the kind of violent chaos his own finished product is specifically, deliberately designed to cause.

**Number 4: The Coward**

There is no single worse label a Khorne cultist can earn anywhere within the faith than this one, and the term itself isn't ever used loosely or casually among true believers. Running from battle counts, in Khorne's own uncompromising theology, as the single ultimate dishonor available to any mortal servant, punished considerably more severely, and considerably more permanently, than simply dying honestly in the attempt ever would be. A cultist marked as a coward after even a single moment of hesitation or genuine retreat, regardless of whatever sound tactical reasoning actually justified the decision at the time, becomes a public example for the rest of the cell rather than simply a private disappointment quietly noted and moved past. They're subjected instead to a punishment specifically designed to demonstrate, publicly, memorably, and unforgettably to everyone forced to watch, exactly what the Blood God actually thinks of self-preservation taking priority over the fight, even for a single, understandable instant under genuinely impossible circumstances that any other faith might have quietly forgiven. Warbands have been documented hunting down their own former members for this exact offense years, sometimes decades after the fact, tracking a single moment of hesitation across entire star systems and treating the resulting debt as considerably more permanent, and considerably more personal, than any ordinary desertion charge an Imperial regiment might bother formally filing against a deserter of its own.

**Number 5: The Hidden Sorcerer**

Every other Chaos God tolerates, or even actively, enthusiastically welcomes followers who dabble in genuine sorcery, Tzeentch above all treating psychic gifts as the single highest honor a mortal servant could possibly offer his patron, an entire theology built specifically around the kind of subtle power Khorne's own faithful are trained from their very first day to instinctively distrust. Khorne does not, and the hatred runs deep enough that Flesh Hounds, daemonic hunting beasts bred and unleashed by his own legions directly, exist for one specific, singular purpose, to track down and utterly destroy psykers wherever they're found in the galaxy, including psykers technically serving loyally within Khorne's own supposed ranks, no exceptions granted for prior service or proven loyalty. A cultist who discovers genuine psychic potential stirring inside themselves while embedded in a Khorne-devoted cell faces an impossible, genuinely no-win choice with no safe option attached to either available side of it. Confess, and face immediate, unhesitating execution from the very brotherhood they've bled and fought alongside for years, men and women who would otherwise die defending them without a second thought. Hide it instead, and live with the constant, exhausting knowledge that a single unguarded moment, one flash of power slipping loose under genuine battlefield stress, ends not just their own life in an instant but likely drags the entire surrounding cell down with them the moment the Flesh Hounds finally, inevitably catch the scent on the wind.

**Number 6: The Bloodletter Summoner**

Somebody within the more ambitious, more desperate Khorne cults eventually has to attempt the single most dangerous ritual their entire faith actually offers anywhere in its sparse, largely unwritten liturgy, tearing open a rift wide enough to pull a Bloodletter, one of Khorne's own Lesser Daemons, directly out of the churning Warp and into cold realspace to fight alongside the cult during whatever engagement demanded such an extreme, last-resort measure in the first place. The genuine, deeply uncomfortable structural irony here is difficult to overstate no matter how you actually look at it. Summoning ritual work is, by any reasonable definition available anywhere in the Imperium's own theological literature, a form of genuine sorcery, precisely the exact practice Khorne despises above almost everything else a mortal servant could possibly attempt in his name, which means the ritualist performing it is committing an act of technical heresy against the very god the entire ritual is nominally meant to honor and please. They can only hope, kneeling in whatever hastily drawn circle they've managed to complete in time, that the end result, a genuine daemon successfully delivered to the battlefield, outweighs the deeply unorthodox method actually used to deliver it there. A botched summoning doesn't simply, quietly fail the way a failed spell might in gentler settings. It tends instead to tear open a considerably less controlled, considerably wider breach than anyone actually intended, and the Bloodletter that eventually emerges from a poorly bound, hastily completed ritual makes absolutely no meaningful distinction between the cultists who personally called it into being and the enemies they originally called it there to help them fight.

**Number 7: The Champion Who Cannot Rest**

This is the fate waiting at the very top of Khorne's own service, and it's precisely the achievement that makes it worst of all. A champion who has genuinely, honestly earned the Blood God's favor across years of faithful service discovers, usually far too late to meaningfully change course by that point, that the actual reward for sustained excellence was never eventual peace or rest. It's an accelerating, ever-steepening demand for considerably more of the exact same thing, since Khorne's own patience for anything resembling genuine rest runs out considerably faster than his hunger for fresh violence ever manages to, and a champion who pauses too long between campaigns doesn't simply stagnate quietly in place. He loses the very blessings that kept him alive, resilient, and effective in the first place, stripped of favor at precisely the moment his own body, worn thin by years of continuous, unrelenting warfare with no real recovery time ever built into the arrangement, needs that specific favor most desperately to keep functioning at anything close to full capacity. Warboss Tuska, the Ork who led a full WAAAGH! into the Eye of Terror itself purely to find something worth actually fighting and fell battling a Daemon Prince of Khorne deep inside it, remains the single most famous exception to the rule, resurrected by Khorne's own direct will every single dawn since, locked in permanent, undying combat against the Blood God's own Bloodletter generals in the shadow of the Brass Citadel. It stands as the rare, celebrated case that proves exactly how genuinely rare actual mercy is anywhere within this particular faith, since Tuska himself was never once a follower to begin with, just an enemy Khorne found entertaining enough to keep around indefinitely. For every single soul granted that kind of rare, genuine mercy, whether loyal follower or simply an enemy who impressed the Blood God enough to earn it, countless thousands of others simply keep fighting, keep bleeding, keep delivering fresh skulls to a throne that has never once, across the entire recorded history of its own steady, patient growth, announced itself finally, mercifully full and closed for further offerings.

So there you have it, seven distinct, thoroughly documented ways to serve a god who genuinely, famously doesn't care where the blood actually comes from, provided it arrives precisely, exactly the way he specifically demands it should. If there's one lesson buried underneath every single entry on this particular list, it's that Khorne's own brutal honesty, his complete, total refusal to dress up the arrangement in false comforting promises the way his rival Chaos gods so frequently, cynically do, somehow makes the entire bargain considerably harder to walk away from rather than easier once you've actually signed on. Every other Chaos power offers its own followers an exit quietly disguised as a further reward down the line. Khorne offers exactly one single reward, endless war without end, and the exit clause was never actually written into the deal at all, not once, not for anyone who has ever taken it. Every warband already knows this going in. Most simply choose to keep fighting anyway, because at some point along the way, the alternative stopped feeling like a real option worth seriously considering at all. Blood for the Blood God has never once, in ten thousand years of continuous war, needed a single follower to actually understand why. It has only ever needed the blood to keep coming. Somewhere out there, right now, another cell is already forming around exactly this same simple, unbreakable premise. Nobody joining it today will get an exit clause either. None of them ever really expected one in the first place. That, in the end, was always the entire point of the arrangement. Blood, honestly given, with nothing held back. Nothing more, nothing less.
